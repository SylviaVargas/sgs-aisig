Recommended AI Tools

The tools below work well for many genealogists today. Focus first on learning what each tool is good at and practice with low‑risk, public material before using it with your own family records.

Important privacy reminder

Avoid uploading documents that contain sensitive information about living people (such as full birth dates, Social Security numbers, or home addresses). Remove or mask sensitive details before sharing material with any AI tool.

Document Transcription & Translation

Tools that help you read and interpret difficult documents.

ChatGPT / Claude (Free & Paid)
  • Transcribe handwritten documents when the writing is reasonably clear.
  • Translate foreign-language records into English.
  • Summarize long documents for your research notes.
  • Suggest possible research leads or next steps.

Starter prompt: “Here is a church record in [language]. Please translate it into English, keeping all names, dates, places, and relationships. Do not guess at words that are unclear—mark them as [illegible].”

MyHeritage Photo Enhancer (Free trial available)
  • Improve blurry or low-contrast photos.
  • Colorize black‑and‑white images.
  • Repair minor damage and enhance facial details.

Usage tip: Upload a photo you already have in your online tree, compare the original and enhanced versions, and record any new details you notice.

Transkribus (Free & Paid)
  • Specialized in historical handwriting recognition.
  • Helpful for long runs of similar documents (census pages, parish registers, letters).
  • Can be trained on specific handwriting for better accuracy.

Usage tip: Start with a small batch of pages, note typical errors, and build a correction routine that works for your records.

Research Organization

Tools that help you summarize notes, timelines, and sources.

NotebookLM (Free)
  • Organizes research notes and documents into one workspace.
  • Creates summaries, timelines, and quick answers based on your own uploaded material.
  • Helps you keep track of what a source says without replacing your analysis.

Starter prompt: “Using only the sources I have provided, summarize everything we know about [Ancestor’s Name] between 1880 and 1910. List each fact with its source.”

Perplexity (Free & Paid)
  • Acts as a research assistant with citations to its web sources.
  • Useful for background context, such as historical events or place descriptions.
  • Helpful for fact‑checking broad claims, but always verify against original records.

Starter prompt: “Give a brief historical overview of [town, county, country] between 1850 and 1900 that might affect migration patterns for local families. Include dates and major events, and list your sources.”

DNA & Pattern Analysis

Tools that use AI within or alongside DNA services to help interpret match lists and relationship patterns. Always confirm suggestions with traditional paper‑trail research.

  • DNA Shared Match Tool – Helps you group and compare shared matches so you can see clusters of related test‑takers.
  • Family Tree Expert – Allows you to upload a GEDCOM file and ask questions about people, relationships, and events in your tree.
  • Relationship charts / “Family Tree Maker”‑style tools – Build and visualize complex family relationships, clarifying how matches may connect.
  • Genetic Data Insight Explorer – Analyzes your raw DNA data (.txt) for patterns and possible relationships.
  • GeneticGenie for Genomics – Focuses on broader genomics analysis that may support health or deep ancestry research.

Caution: AI‑assisted DNA tools can suggest likely relationships, but they are not proof on their own. Use them as clues and confirm proposed connections with documentary evidence and standard genealogical proof practices.
